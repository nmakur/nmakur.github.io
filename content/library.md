## The Algebraist's Cookbook

### About

My friend <a href="https://www.rtrosten.com/">Robert</a> has a project he dubbed the <a href="https://www.rtrosten.com/notes">Analyst's Cookbook</a>. I was inspired, and so decided to start an Algebraist's Cookbook. My goals for this project were
- <color val="green">Self-containment</color>: Everything stated should be proven, starting with the basics (within reason - I'm not going to give a proof of the classification of simple groups). It always annoyed me that most algebra classes state but do not prove most of these results:
  - The simplicity of *A<sub>n</sub>* for *n &ge; 5*.
  - Burnside's theorem that groups of order *p<sup>a</sup>q<sup>b</sup>* are solvable
  - The Nielsen-Schreier theorem that subgroups of free groups are again free
  - Wedderburn's theorem that a finite division ring is a field

- <color val="green"> Completeness</color>: Expand the reader's horizons by including results like
  - Hall's theorem on Hall &pi;-subgroups in solvable groups
  - The Schur-Zassenhaus theorem on complements within finite groups
  - Purely inseperable and transcendental field extensions
  - Infinite Galois theory
  - Noncommutative ring theory
- <color val="green">Interconnectedness</color>: Highlight connections that one doesn't typically see in a first algebra class. In particular, emphasizing universal properties and appealing to a category-theoretic point of view throughout. This means that this isn't a great first exposition to algebra, but should be useful to someone with a first course looking to explore.

I compiled results and proofs from many sources for this project. Some that I used heavily are: course notes from CMU's algebra classes, *Algebra: Chapter 0* by Aluffi, *Algebra* by Issacs.

## My Notebook

### Brief Expositions

<color val="red">Ultrafilters and Applications (WIP)</color>: An introduction to ultrafilters and their uses in topology and combinatorics.

[Projective Geometry](/library/Projective_Geometry.pdf): A brief introduction to projective geometry and its applications, including a proof that all 2-variable quadratic curves are conic sections. Based on a class I took at MathILy 2022.

[Topological Combinatorics](/library/Topological_Combinatorics.pdf): Topics in topological combinatorics, including Sperner's Lemma, the Borsuk-Ulam Theorem, and Monsky's Theorem.

<color val="orange">Primes and Zeta Functions</color>: An introduction to analytic number theory, based on the class of the same name that I took at PROMYS 2023. I'm not posting it publicly, but will gladly send it to someone who [contacts me](/contact/) with proof that they're affiliated with PROMYS.

[The Fundamental Theorem of Finitely Generated Abelian Groups](/library/Fundamental_Theorem_of_Finitely_Generated_Abelian_Groups.pdf): A proof of the fundamental theorem given by viewing a finitely generated group as a quotient of a free group and finding the Smith Normal Form of a corresponding matrix.

[Special Relativity](/library/Special_Relativity.pdf): A brief introduction to special relativity, based on <a href="https://www.coursera.org/learn/einstein-relativity">this class</a>.

[Proofs of Irrationality](/library/Proofs_of_Irrationality.pdf): Proofs that your favorite numbers are irrational.

[Sums of Squares](/library/Sums_of_Squares.pdf): Which integers can be written as the sum of two squares, and why all integers are the sum of four.

[Intersections of Hyperplanes](/library/Intersections_of_Hyperplanes.pdf): How many pieces can *n* codimension-1 hyperplanes divide *d*-dimensional space into?

[Hall's Matching Problem](/library/Hall_Matching_Problem.pdf): Three proofs of Hall's Matching Theorem.
