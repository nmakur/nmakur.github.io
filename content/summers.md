## 2024: PROMYS

<div style="display: flex; gap: 24px; align-items: flex-start; margin: 2rem 0;">
<div style="flex: 1;">

Half of my PROMYS 2023 friend group returned in 2024, which was a lot of fun. I was in a bit of a funny position of talking a lot to both the counselors (since my 2023 friends were counselors) and the students (since I was a student), which also meant that I made good friends in both groups. Math-wise, I wasn't super excited by dynamical systems and I knew most of Galois theory, so the advanced seminars weren't the best fit for me (it's also a bit hard to live up to Li-Mei's Primes and Zeta Functions). I also didn't do a research lab, which I definitely regret. The counselors held *a ton* of counselor seminars, though, and so I was able to learn a lot of math that was actually quite useful going into college (especially category theory and representation theory). I'm glad I went, though I definitely had a better time at PROMYS 2023.

## 2023: PROMYS

<div style="display: flex; gap: 24px; align-items: flex-start; margin: 2rem 0;">
<div style="flex: 0 0 auto;">
{% set promys_23_photo= resize_image(path="images/promys_23.jpg", width=400, height=600, op="fit") %}

  style="border: 1px solid var(--cyan-color);"/>

<div style="flex: 1;">

I think PROMYS 2023 was the best summer of my life (at least, at the time of writing). I made an amazing group of friends, all of whom I still consider to be some of my best friends today. We did just about everything together, including math (obviously), exploring Boston, seeing a bilingual production of *Hamlet*, and watching Barbenheimer. We also all stayed up a bit too late together each night doing math and playing fish, which wasn't the best for our sleep but was great for bonding.

Math-wise, PROMYS made me *wayy* better at math. I had so much fun with the daily number theory psets, and my counselor was amazing and gave me followup questions on all my work which allowed me to explore a ton beyond what was covered in the problems (if/when I return as a counselor, I aspire to be like her). I also loved the Primes & Zeta Functions advanced seminar I took, it was almost as well-designed as the NT psets, and at times even more interesting. Spending 6 weeks straight hammering away really just makes you better at problem solving than you could ever imagine.

## 2022: MathILy

<div style="display: flex; gap: 24px; align-items: flex-start; margin: 2rem 0;">
<div style="flex: 1;">

This was my first time actually experiencing a mathematical community. I had a ton of fun collaborating with others who were actually passionate about math, and also generally just talking with them and hearing what they had to say. Some of my favorite memories: proving rank-nullity from scratch on the first or second day (I didn't realize I could), spending 3 straight days trying to come up with a proof of Hall's theorem (until I finally did), and getting humbled when my friend Lily found a proof of Hall's theorem in about an hour the next day by going down a path that I was adamant did not work (MathILy was a very developmental experience for my character).
</div>

I also made a bunch of great friends. We would hang out in a garden every night and just talk for an hour. During the afternoons, we'd go into the lecture building (which was air-conditioned) and work or talk the entire time. This became a pretty popular idea, and I ended up getting to know almost everyone in the program. Most of us became pretty tight-knit by the end, and I'm extremely glad to have met everyone who was there: I think those friendships did more for me than any math I actually learned.
